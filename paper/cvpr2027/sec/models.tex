In faces (Table~\ref{tab:models}), LaMa with face-trained weights is at \mfLamaHqCelebaMse{} in CelebA and \mfLamaHqImdbMse{} in IMDb-WIKI under \ac{MSE} (95\% intervals \mfLamaHqCelebaMseCi{} and \mfLamaHqImdbMseCi{}), with scene-trained weights at \mfLamaPlacesCelebaMse{} and \mfLamaPlacesImdbMse{}, and the diffusion model at \mfSdCelebaMse{} and \mfSdImdbMse{}: all closer than every reference, including the person's own second photograph (\mfLamaHqCelebaRefRepeatMse{} and \mfLamaHqImdbRefRepeatMse{}).
The fills are not averaged: the norm of their individual detail is \mfFaceDetailMin{} to \mfFaceDetailMax{} of the target's (median), against \mfLamaHqCelebaRefNearestDetail{} for the mean of the 16 nearest people.
Yet the models are only \mfFaceAheadMin{} to \mfFaceAheadMax{} ahead of that mean (\mfLamaHqCelebaRefNearestMse{} and \mfLamaHqImdbRefNearestMse{}), which uses no model and nothing of the person beyond the surroundings.
In the brain, the challenge-winning ensemble is at \mfBratsQtimMse{} \mfBratsQtimMseCi{} on \mfBratsQtimN{} of 221 people: ahead of the mean of the 16 nearest people (\mfBratsQtimRefNearestMse{}) and the dataset average (\mfBratsQtimRefMeanMse{}), while the person's second scan stays twice as close (\mfBratsQtimRefRepeatMse{} on the same people and cubes).
A low error at this size therefore shows that a fill continues its surroundings, which a model sees in full, and not that it kept the person.
Fed the frame without upsampling, LaMa is level with that mean (Supplementary Section~S4).
