\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:synthesis}
The conditional mean of the hidden content given the context minimizes expected squared error; this is the distortion side of the perception-distortion tradeoff~\cite{blau2018}.
A fill drawn from the true conditional distribution has twice that error, because it adds an independent draw of the unpredictable part.
The softened same person and the mean of the 16 nearest people are empirical stand-ins for that mean.
Under \ac{MSE} this reward is exact: if the second image $s$ has as much individual detail as the target, the softened version is at $1-Q(s)/4$ times its distance, so T2 shows only its size.
The ladder adds that size on real anatomy: no error or similarity metric places the person's own second image ahead of its softened version in any image set (Table~\ref{tab:tests}), and set-level metrics trade this reward for the opposite blind spot.

\paragraph{A recipe for inpainting benchmarks.}
For each hidden region, a benchmark can compute the region's dataset average over unrelated people, the mean of the 16 people whose context rings are closest, and 16 random people, all using the same valid pixels as the submitted fills.
Scores are then reported on the random-person scale of Eq.~\ref{eq:anchors}, so that a submission at 0.5 is as close as the dataset average, and its gap to a context search is read against that set's own nearest-people score (0.20 to 0.40 at 1/8).
Where a dataset has second images of the same people, the softened second image adds a direct test of whether the metric rewards averaging, and a set-level metric on the same fills shows whether the submission has lost realism.

Next to the references, a model score becomes readable (Table~\ref{tab:models}): most of a model's score in faces is reachable without a model, and the brain model is still twice as far as the second scan.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The ladder calibrates metrics and does not by itself rank models.
The softened control is the error-optimal shrinkage of the second image, so T2 shows a reward for shrinkage toward the average, which also removes acquisition differences; it does not isolate lost anatomy.
The pretrained models run mostly outside their training domain, with one diffusion sample per region and subsets of people for the two slow models.
Residual misalignment is the main confounder outside faces: it adds to every distance in brain, retina, and iris, most in mirrored comparisons with the opposite side.
Misalignment alone does not explain the averaging reward, because faces, aligned on landmarks, fail T2 in the same way (Table~\ref{tab:tests}).
The cross-dataset summaries and tests were defined after the per-dataset results and are exploratory; only 23 QTIM people have a second scan, two of nine image sets record families, and the set-level exclusion rule was fixed after seeing one value.
\ac{LPIPS}, topology and set-level metrics still use about 1/5 of the object in faces, retina, and iris, and \ac{FID} applies Inception features to small gray patches, far from their training domain~\cite{kynkaanniemi2023fid}.
The ladder measures closeness to known references, not clinical or biometric correctness: a fill at the same person's position can still miss a lesion.

\paragraph{Ethics and data.}
We collected no new data and use nine public image sets under their terms: QTIM and GRAPE (CC0), IXI (CC BY-SA 3.0), PAPILA (GPL 3.0+), CelebA (non-commercial research only), IMDb-WIKI (academic research only), and UTIRIS and UPOL (free with citation).
QTIM, GRAPE and PAPILA report ethics approval and signed informed consent; CelebA and IMDb-WIKI are Internet images of public figures (Supplementary Section~S2).
Faces, irises and retinas are biometric, so we show one QTIM brain scan (CC0) and otherwise only population averages or maps.
The nearest-person search is a form of biometric retrieval; we run it only within each image set and report aggregate distances.
We did not balance the image sets by sex, age or ancestry, so the distances describe these cohorts, not clinical populations.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
References of known relationship turn a single inpainting score into a position on a ladder from the same person to a random one.
Across nine image sets, error and similarity metrics reward averaging; set-level metrics barely separate the right person from a random one; and pretrained models score close to a retrieval baseline that requires no model.
The three reference scores need no model or labels and show where a new model's score sits.
