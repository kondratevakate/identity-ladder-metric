How much of a person is left in a synthesized image?

BraTS inpainting and most image synthesis work score a fill with RMSE, PSNR and SSIM against the true image. These
numbers say how close the pixels are. They do not say whether the fill still looks like *this* brain, or like any
plausible brain. This repository builds a calibrated score for that question and tests where the standard metrics
fail.

The idea is a ladder of pairs with known amounts of shared identity:

![Identity ladder with faces](results/figures/identity_ladder_faces.png)

*Drawn faces, no real person. Top: the person; bottom: the image it is compared with. In MRI the same rungs are a
re-scan, another scanner or protocol, a later visit, the other hemisphere, a co-twin or sibling, a stranger and the
average of other people. RMSE is taken inside the dashed box over one brow and eye, the analogue of an inpainting
mask; for the other side the box sits on the other eye and is mirrored before comparison. The blurred average (0.170) comes out
closer than the sibling (0.193), although the sibling plainly shares the person's features.
Generated by `src/ladder_faces.py`.*

The population mean is not a person, but it has to sit on the ladder: under squared error it lands exactly halfway
between "identical" and "stranger" ($\rho = 1/2$), whatever the person looks like. A sibling who is not a twin also
differs in age and scan date, so in practice the sibling rung sits a little above DZ.

Why the sibling sits *behind* the mean: under squared error a sibling's tissue has $\rho = 1 - h^2/2$ (it shares on
average half of the genetic part), and the mean always has $\rho = 1/2$. The sibling would need $h^2 > 1$ to win, which
is impossible. So under MSE every sibling loses to the average, whatever the heritability, and even an MZ co-twin wins
only when $h^2 > 1/2$. Real tissue from a relative, which truly shares anatomy, is penalised more than a blur. This is
the order the metric produces, not the order of identity, and it is the problem this project is about.

![Identity ladder](results/figures/identity_ladder.png)

*Expected distance of each pair under the Gaussian variance-component model, as a ratio to matched strangers. Left:
genetic share 0.54, the MZ co-twin is closer than the mean. Right: genetic share 0.19, the mean overtakes the MZ
co-twin. Generated by `src/ladder_diagram.py`.*

A useful distance should keep this order. We express every distance on one scale:

$$c(d) = P\left(d_{\text{stranger}} > d\right)$$

where strangers are unrelated pairs taken under the *same* nuisance condition (same
camera pair, sensor, protocol). $c = 0.5$ is a typical stranger, $c = 1$ is closer than every stranger. The mean $c$ over a
rung is the AUC of that rung against strangers. It is the same move as the random-pair calibration of TM-score in
protein structure.

Status: research code, work in progress (Kate Kondrateva, Hertie Institute for AI in Brain Health, Tübingen). Pilots
use small samples. Nothing here is a validated clinical or biometric measure.

## Why this matters: BraTS inpainting rewards regression to the mean

This is the result that started the project.

**What the challenge itself reports.** In the 2023 BraTS inpainting overview [1], the ranking by SSIM, RMSE and PSNR
was clear. The top team (Ying-Weng et al.: SSIM 0.91, RMSE 0.07, PSNR 23.59) won. The authors note that apart from
one 2D diffusion model, which had stripe artefacts in the sagittal and coronal views, the methods "produce blurry
inpaintings across all three views". Looking at the images "painted a more faceted picture" than the ranking. They
call for synthesis metrics that agree better with expert perception. The 2025 winner is again a U-Net trained to
inpaint masked regions (SSIM 0.919, PSNR 26.93, RMSE 0.052 on the final test set) [2].

**What the downstream check showed.** The organisers expected that a downstream U-Net segmentation of the inpainted
brains would expose the blurry fills. It did the opposite: the segmentation of the blurred fills came *closer* to the
oracle segmentation of the real image.

**Why both happen. It is arithmetic, not a bug in the models.** Write the tissue as $x = \mu + \delta$: $\mu$ is the
population average, $\delta$ everything individual (genes, development, side, session, noise), with variance $V$ per
voxel, and $h^2$ the genetic share of $V$. The expected squared error of a fill is then:

| Fill | Error against the truth | Expected squared error |
|---|---|---|
| population mean $\mu$ | $\delta$ | $V$ |
| stranger's tissue | $\delta - \delta'$ (independent) | $2V$ |
| DZ co-twin's or sibling's tissue | half of the genetic part is shared | $2V - h^2 V$ |
| MZ co-twin's tissue | non-shared part only | $2V(1 - h^2)$ |

So the mean always has half the error of a real stranger's tissue. It beats a sibling always and an MZ co-twin whenever
$h^2 < 1/2$. Squared error is minimised exactly at the conditional mean, so a network
trained or selected on MSE, RMSE or PSNR is pushed towards blur by the metric itself. SSIM does the same in
homogeneous regions through its stabilising constant $C_2$. In the synthetic run the measured error ratio of the
mean against a stranger is 0.50, against 0.504 predicted.

Segmentation is a pointwise score too. Dice and voxel accuracy against the oracle count voxel matches, and when a fill
carries no individual anatomy, the best guess for each voxel is the most typical label: the mode instead of the mean.
A blurred fill pushes the segmenter towards exactly that typical boundary. A realistic fill with someone else's folding
puts boundaries in confident but wrong places and is penalised. So the honest fill loses twice:

- in intensity, under MSE/PSNR, because of the mean;
- in labels, under Dice against the oracle, because of the mode.

A downstream check with a pointwise metric does not remove the bias. It inherits it (hypothesis H19).

**What would break it.**

- Treat the population mean as the floor, not the stranger. A fill means something only if it beats the mean, so
  report its distance from the mean next to its distance from the truth.
- Score the shape and topology of the segmentation instead of overlap. Blur simplifies folding: fewer sulci, a
  different Euler characteristic, smoother boundaries.
- Calibrate on the identity ladder in label space: re-scan, co-twin and stranger donor fills next to the mean.
- Check realism at the population level: can the fill be told apart from real tissue at all?

The synthetic experiments below test each step of this argument.

Challenge: Local Synthesis of Healthy Brain Tissue via Inpainting. arXiv:2305.08992v3, 2024.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08992

[2] Zhang J, Weng Y, Chen K. Robust 3D Brain MRI Inpainting with Random Masking Augmentation. arXiv:2511.20202, 2025.

## Main finding so far: the average beats real tissue

On squared-error metrics, an identity-free **population mean** fill can outrank real tissue of the same family, and
stays near the top even when all individual detail is gone. This section shows the cases; the plots with all metrics
are in the draft collection [`docs/figures_draft.md`](docs/figures_draft.md).

### The cases: what each fill looks like

![Synthetic tissue: donors and the mean](results/figures/visual_tissue_rebuilt.png)

*One synthetic target with every donor: repeat capture, later visit, other hemisphere, MZ co-twin, stranger and the
population mean. Middle row: the filled region enlarged. Bottom row: threshold segmentation. The mean is a grey blur
with no folding of its own, yet on RMSE it scores c = 1.00 against strangers, above the MZ (0.71) and DZ (0.67)
co-twins at genetic share 0.19.*

![Synthetic eyes: donors and the mean](results/figures/visual_eyes_rebuilt.png)

*The same set of donors for a synthetic eye.*

### Removing the individual step by step

$$y_\lambda = \mu + \lambda\,(x - \mu), \qquad \lambda = 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0$$

24 targets over 3 seeds per modality.

| Modality | Candidate | RMSE c | SSIM c |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| tissue | repeat capture | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| tissue | 50% residual | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| tissue | population mean | 0.995 | 0.950 |
| tissue | wrong detail (noise of the same size) | 0.566 | 0.184 |
| eyes | repeat capture | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| eyes | 50% residual | 0.998 | 1.000 |
| eyes | population mean | 0.797 | 0.835 |
| eyes | wrong detail | 0.389 | 0.085 |

Half of the target's individual signal can disappear and RMSE/SSIM do not notice. Distance from the mean catches the
collapse, but the noise control shows that being far from the mean is not the same as being the right person.
Full numbers and caveats: [`results/population_null_report.md`](results/population_null_report.md).

### All BraTS metrics, not only RMSE and SSIM

All nine outputs of the official package: RMSE, MSE, MAE, MSLE, PSNR, PSNR_eps, PSNR_01, PSNR_01_eps, SSIM. Table:
[`results/brats_metrics_table.csv`](results/brats_metrics_table.csv) (56 rows), combined with the label ladder in
[`results/all_ladder_metrics.csv`](results/all_ladder_metrics.csv) (1,116 rows).

Facts about the official code that matter here (`src/brats_panel_checks.py`): SSIM is computed per 2D slice; plain
PSNR clamps the fill to the reference range; within one case MSE, PSNR_eps, PSNR_01 and PSNR_01_eps rank fills
identically.

### Segmentation and topology (H19)

Does a downstream segmentation (Dice) or the topology of the mask also reward the mean? Synthetic two-class tissue,
threshold segmenter, the segmentation of the true image as oracle.

![Synthetic tissue with segmentations](results/figures/label_fixed_example.png)

![Folding-like anatomy](results/figures/label_fixed_example_folding.png)

![Other-hemisphere donor locations](results/figures/label_hemisphere_locations.png)

Five seeds, two genetic shares, three prior strengths:

- RMSE c of the mean stays at 0.995 to 0.997 everywhere.
- The Dice advantage of the mean over the MZ co-twin is **not** universal. With a low genetic share and a strong
  prior it is positive in every seed (+0.215). With a weak prior it crosses zero, and with no prior there is no
  consistent direction. With genetic share 0.54 the MZ twin wins on Dice in every seed.
- Component counts of the mean score about like a stranger (c 0.50 to 0.56). Holes are too rare to tell anything.

Details: [`results/label_robustness_report.md`](results/label_robustness_report.md).

### Pupil dilation: same eye, scored as a stranger

![Synthetic pupil example](results/figures/pupil_fixed_example.png)

A +30% pupil dilation keeps every identity parameter of the simulated eye, yet scores RMSE c = 0.004 and SSIM
c = 0.006 against normal-pupil strangers. Matching the strangers' dilation brings it only to about 0.45. The
population mean scores 0.79 (RMSE) and 0.84 (SSIM). Details: [`results/pupil_report.md`](results/pupil_report.md).

## Pilots on open data

Earlier calibrated pilots, small n, each re-derived by an independent audit
([`results/audit_2026-11-01.json`](results/audit_2026-11-01.json)).

**GRAPE fundus** ([`results/grape_calibration.json`](results/grape_calibration.json)), camera-pair matched reference:

| Rung | RETFound c | pixels c |
|---|---|---|
| same eye, other visit, same camera | 0.873 [0.832, 0.906] | 0.714 |
| same eye, other visit, other camera | 0.777 [0.735, 0.812] | 0.669 |
| fellow eye, same visit | 0.907 [0.883, 0.930] | 0.692 |

A camera change costs RETFound about 0.10 in c. With a pooled "any other camera" reference it looked like 0.26,
which is why the reference has to match the nuisance condition exactly.

**Iris, UPOL and UTIRIS** ([`results/iris_calibration.json`](results/iris_calibration.json),
[`results/iris_codes.json`](results/iris_codes.json)): a nuisance-only control (mean and SD of grey level, two
numbers per image) reproduces most of the pixel scores. On these sets pixel similarity is mostly exposure. The
classical iris code separates the same eye well (c 0.91) and puts the fellow eye at 0.68.

**MRI image-space ladder** ([`results/image_ladder.json`](results/image_ladder.json)): SIMON and IXI, brain-to-brain
registration to MNI2009a. Medians grow in ladder order, from same session to strangers at another centre.

**SIMON E0** ([`results/simon_e0.json`](results/simon_e0.json)): scanner against time and the mirror axis in one
travelling-subject dataset.

## Limits

- Synthetic results come from a hand-designed generator and a threshold segmenter. They show what the metrics *can*
  do, not what they do on real brains.
- Seed ranges in the reports are min/max of seed means, not confidence intervals.
- Real-image demonstrations so far are one MRI and one iris case. No BraTS data, winner models or FastSurfer runs
  yet.
