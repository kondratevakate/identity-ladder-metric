\section{The softened control under squared error}
\label{sec:s-t2}
\input{tab/t2_identity_numbers}
Under \ac{MSE}, the position of the softened same person follows from the position of the unchanged second image, as the Discussion of the main text states.
This section gives the derivation and compares it with the measured values.

Let $d=x-\hat\mu$ be the individual detail of the target and $g=f-\hat\mu$ that of the second image, on the valid pixels of one hidden region.
The softened fill is $\hat\mu+\lambda g$, so its squared error is
\begin{equation}
\|d-\lambda g\|^2=\|d\|^2-2\lambda\langle d,g\rangle+\lambda^2\|g\|^2 ,
\end{equation}
and the error of the unchanged second image, $\|d-g\|^2$, gives $2\langle d,g\rangle=\|d\|^2+\|g\|^2-\|d-g\|^2$.
Summing over targets and regions and dividing by $\sum\|d\|^2$, which is the error of the dataset average in Eq.~1 of the main text, we obtain
\begin{equation}
Q_{\mathrm{soft}}=1-\lambda\,(1+G-Q)+\lambda^2 G,\qquad G=\frac{\sum\|g\|^2}{\sum\|d\|^2},
\label{eq:s-t2}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ is the value of the unchanged second image and $G$ says how much individual detail the second images carry relative to the targets.
No assumption about noise or independence enters.
With $\lambda=1-Q/2$, the value used for the softened control, and $G=1$, Eq.~\ref{eq:s-t2} becomes $Q_{\mathrm{soft}}=Q\,(1-Q/4)$.
The softened image is then at $1-Q/4$ times the distance of the unchanged one, on the $Q$ scale and on the random-person scale alike, whatever the anatomy.

Table~\ref{tab:s-t2} compares this prediction with the measured positions in the \tTwoSets{} image sets that have a second image.
The two agree within \tTwoMaxDiff{}, and the value of $G$ that makes Eq.~\ref{eq:s-t2} exact lies between 0.95 and 1.05.
Under \ac{MSE}, test T2 therefore cannot pass in these image sets, and its result shows only the size of the reward for softening, which grows with $Q$.
No such identity holds for \ac{SSIM}, the tolerance scores or \ac{LPIPS}, so for these metrics T2 is an empirical result.
